## Past


[Past]
Flames devour the night.
A small village lies in ruin — homes collapsing, cries for help fading into the roar of the blaze.
Through the smoke runs a young dragonborn boy, clutching a singed wooden toy and a half-burned loaf of bread. His eyes sting, his lungs ache, but he runs — because someone told him to run and don’t look back.

He trips, scraping his claws against the dirt, and for a moment he looks back anyway.
A woman — human, wrapped in soot and courage — shouts for survivors. She locks eyes with him, reaching out a hand through the smoke.

He runs toward her.
Then the roof caves in.

When he wakes, there’s only silence. The woman’s voice is gone, and the toy in his claws is blackened by flame.
He stares at the smoldering embers of what once was home.

“I’ll find a family that can’t burn.”

The ember glows faintly in reply.


## Present


The glow of fire again — but this time, controlled.
Torches flicker against the brass insignia of the Coinfire Consortium. Tarhun stands at the head of a polished war table, a leader now — steady, respected, commanding.

Reports scatter the table — maps, numbers, orders signed with his seal. His soldiers stand at attention, awaiting their next mission.

“Go. Bring them home. Protect the village.”

They salute and depart.

Time folds.
The next scene opens on the same hall — quiet now, empty except for the faint hiss of rain on embers. A scout kneels before him, armor streaked with ash.

“It seems it’s all burned, my lord. The village… it’s gone.”

The air turns heavy.
The torches flicker lower.
Outside the open doors, smoke rises — the same color as the one that took his first home.

Tarhun doesn’t move. The only sound is the faint crackle of the fire, whispering — or perhaps mocking — that old promise:

“A family that can’t burn.”
