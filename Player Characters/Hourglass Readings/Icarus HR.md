## Past

🌙 Moonshadow Hourglass Vision – “The Rubyheart Trial”

(Icarus Inkwell’s Past Vision)

The silver sand swirls, catching the moonlight as Maelis’s fingertips brush the glass.

“The past remembers,” she murmurs. “Even when the heart tries to forget.”

The world folds inward — light bending, air thinning — until you stand once more within the marble halls of the Grand Arcanum Artifact Museum.

You’re thirteen again.
The ceilings are impossibly high, the air warm with candlelight and curiosity. The scent of old wood polish and sea salt drifts from the nearby docks.

A familiar voice:

“Icarus, look at this one!”

Aphrodite Ashwood stands by a gleaming display — a glass case holding a necklace strung with a ruby the size of a plum, glowing faintly from within. She leans close, her reflection doubling in the crystal walls.

“It’s beautiful,” she whispers. “Can you imagine? Owning something like that. I’d wear it every day, even to bed.”

You laugh, teasing, but she’s serious — wistful, not greedy. You’ve always known she doesn’t have much. Her father works the docks; her mother’s been gone for years. The museum is her escape — and your shared sanctuary.

Then, voices echo from a nearby chamber. A tour group, led by a guide in a crimson sash, describing the enchantments protecting the relics. You can’t help yourself — curiosity pulls you toward the sound, your mind already racing with notes and details to record later.

You drift into the next room — where ancient armor stands on display, plates polished to a mirror sheen, runes faintly shifting across the surface. You lean in, fascinated.

Then — a crash.

A high, crystalline shatter. The sound of alarms, sharp and sudden.

Sigils flare to life on the walls — red, pulsing, alive.

You run.

The hall explodes into chaos — guards shouting, people backing away from the central display. And there, at the center — Aphrodite, crumpled to the floor, surrounded by shards of glass that glitter like ice in the sunlight.

The ruby necklace lies across her chest, the chain tangled in her hair. Her hands are bleeding.

“I—I don’t know what happened!” she cries, as the guards rush in. “It just—broke—and I fainted—I didn’t touch it!”

The guards exchange grim looks. One kneels, lifting the ruby with a gloved hand; another gestures to the shattered frame.

“She was closest to the case,” he mutters. “No one else near enough to break the seal.”

You open your mouth to protest, but someone grabs your shoulder — pulling you back from the scene. You never saw it happen.

The vision flickers — time skips.

Now: a courthouse, dimly lit by oil lamps.
Aphrodite stands small and pale before the bench. Her wrists tremble as she clasps them together.

“She was found wearing the stolen artifact,” a guard reports flatly. “Glass fragments on her hands and clothing. No other suspects were seen near the display.”

Another guard hesitates, glancing down at his notes.

“There were… additional smudged prints on the case, Your Honor. But unclear, partial. Could’ve been from cleaning staff, or the crowd before her.”

The judge, robed in indigo, frowns. His gavel rests heavy in his hand.

“And no evidence of tampering by magic?”

“None we could detect, Your Honor.”

Aphrodite shakes her head, desperate.

“Please… I didn’t take it. I don’t even know how it got on me. I just remember light, and then—glass. Please, I didn’t!”

The judge sighs. The courtroom murmurs.

“Given the testimony, and her proximity to the artifact…”

He raises the gavel.

“Guilty. The accused is hereby banished from the Grand Arcanum district, until her twenty-first year. May this serve as mercy, given her age.”

Bang.

The sound echoes like thunder through the vision.

Aphrodite turns, her tear-streaked face meeting your eyes —

“You believe me, don’t you?”

The words ripple through the falling sand.
The courtroom fades into silver light, and the last thing you see is a single grain of red — falling slower than the rest.

Maelis’s voice returns, soft, almost reverent:

“The truth of the past is not always what was seen… but what was lost in the seeing.”




## Future


🌙 Moonshadow Hourglass Vision – “Shadows of the Future”

The silver sand shivers and swirls in the Hourglass, catching the full moon’s glow. Maelis brushes her fingers along its edge, her whisper soft:

“Watch closely. The future is never fully revealed at once.”

Scene 1 – The Streets:

The air is damp and cool. Lanterns flicker in misty alleys as a girl darts from stall to stall. She moves like a shadow, nimble and practiced, lifting food and small trinkets unnoticed. Her eyes glint with a strange familiarity, though you cannot place why.

She slips into the edge of the nearby woods, moving with the skill of a hunter — tracking small game, gathering herbs, storing what she takes in a carefully packed satchel.

Scene 2 – The Young Leader:

The vision leaps forward. The girl is older now, in her late teens or early twenties. She stands at the center of a secluded courtyard, surrounded by a circle of young people. They watch her with trust, reverence, and curiosity.

She hands out food, distributes supplies, and offers guidance — teaching survival, stealth, and cunning. At the edges, shadows cling to her movements: she still steals, hunts, and organizes covert trades. Every action deliberate, every plan meticulous.

Scene 3 – The Tent of Papers:

She slips into a large tent, canvas worn and smelling of dust and ink. Tables are stacked with papers, maps, and scattered scrolls. With practiced fingers, she selects sheets and hides them beneath her coat.

Later, she is seen on a street, carefully selling these same papers to those who recognize their value. The location, the details, the documents themselves — hazy, yet familiar, as if drawn from some memory half-forgotten.

Scene 4 – The Wedding Glimpse:

The vision shifts again. You are standing at a celebration, bright with lanterns and music. Familiar faces smile, voices echo, laughter fills the air. You feel the weight of the moment — this seems to be your own wedding.

At the edge of the crowd, a figure sits quietly in a shadowed corner. She is familiar — her posture, her gaze, the tilt of her head — yet you cannot place her fully. She watches, silent, then vanishes into the crowd before you can move.

The sand settles, silver flowing steadily, the vision fading.
